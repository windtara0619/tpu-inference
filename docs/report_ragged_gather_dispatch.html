The Raggedness Blind Spot

ragged_gather_reduce picks the SparseCore kernel over the dense TensorCore fallback using one signal — whether x is bigger than 60% of VMEM. Benchmarking the two implementations directly, on identical inputs, across a regular 26×10 grid of size and row-validity (260 cases, size in 0.1× steps up to 2× the threshold then coarser out to 16×, validity in clean 10% steps from 10% to 100%), shows that signal alone gets the call wrong 126 times — and every single miss runs the same direction. A diagnostic hook added to the real dispatcher and run against live traffic shows why that hasn't caused a problem yet.

134 / 260 cases where the size-only heuristic picked the actually-faster implementation
4.68× slowdown at the worst misfire — heuristic chose SparseCore at 1.3× the threshold, 90% valid, fallback was faster
126 / 126 misfires that are the heuristic over-committing to SparseCore — not one goes the other way on this grid
~44–49% valid-row fraction where the crossover settles once size clears ~3× the threshold, independent of size beyond that

What the sweep shows

01

At 10% valid the crossover sits right at 1.1× — by 20% it's already past 2×

At 10% valid, fallback wins every step from 0.1× up through 1.0× the threshold, then SparseCore takes over right at 1.1× and holds it the rest of the way — a clean, single crossing. Raise validity to just 20% and that crossing disappears from the entire fine-grained 0.1–2.0× range: fallback wins all twenty of those steps, and SparseCore only takes over once the next data point, 3×, is reached. The same holds at 30% and 40% valid. Moving validity from 10% to 20% pushes the crossover multiplier from ~1.1× to somewhere past 2× — the crossover is far more sensitive to validity than to size in this region.

02

Past ~3×, the crossover stops moving and settles near 45% valid

For every size from 3× to 16× the threshold, SparseCore wins at 40% valid and fallback wins at 50% valid — the same bracket, six multiplier-steps in a row spanning a 5.3× range in raw size. Interpolating the two puts the true crossover at roughly 44–49% valid for all of them, tightening around ~47%. This is the shape you'd expect if SparseCore's per-call cost is dominated by a fixed start-up term at small sizes but becomes purely linear in valid-row count once the input is large enough: the crossover valid-fraction is then just the ratio of two per-row constants, independent of size. It also matches the earlier, coarser sweep's estimate almost exactly — a useful cross-check between two independently sampled grids.

03

On this grid, every single misfire runs the same direction

All 126 of 126 misfires are the heuristic over-committing to SparseCore on inputs that turned out too dense for it — not one goes the other way. The worst is 4.68× the optimal time, at 1.3× the threshold and 90% valid (0.104ms fallback vs 0.487ms SparseCore). That 0-for-126 is partly a property of this grid, though: it only samples validity down to 10%, and the earlier, finer sweep (which went to 2% and 5% valid) did find a handful of rare misses in the other direction down there, the worst a much smaller 1.51×. Either way the asymmetry is stark: a validity-aware threshold would mostly need to guard against over-dispatching to SparseCore, not under-dispatching.

Real valid_fraction is pinned at 12.5%, in every run, regardless of decode/prefill mix

Across all three captures — two decode-dominated, one prefill-dominated, wildly different dispatch mixes (2% to 77% SparseCore share) — every single out_size bucket in every run has mean and median valid_fraction of 0.125, with ≥45% valid showing up in at most 1% of calls anywhere. That's exactly 1 / topk for this model (Qwen3-30B-A3B routes topk=8): the mask here is over the topk-expanded row set, so on average 1-in-8 rows validates per chunk regardless of chunk size or decode/prefill split. It's a structural constant of the routing config, not a property of the workload.

05

Zero misfires observed — because real traffic never reaches the risky zone

Cross-referencing every distinct size_multiplier seen in production against the benchmark's valid_fraction≈0.1 column: 0.833× and below stayed fallback, matching the measured crossover at ~1.1× for that validity; 1.667× and above went SparseCore, also correct, all the way out to 26.7×. Every dispatch decision logged across 513,408 real calls in three different traffic shapes was the right one. Not because the heuristic is robust in general — the sweep above shows plainly that it isn't — but because topk=8 routing keeps real validity far from the 20–90% band where the heuristic actually misfires. A model or routing config with denser expert assignment would be the one to watch.

Reading on this

The current condition, size(x) × dtype_bytes × 2 < vmem_capacity_bytes × 0.6, encodes only size. The data suggests a two-part replacement: below ~3× the size threshold, keep a size-scaled minimum-validity gate, since SparseCore only pays off there when very few rows are valid and the required fraction rises quickly with size. Above ~3×, size stops mattering and a single fixed cutoff on valid_fraction around 0.45 tracks the real crossover for every size tested, up to 16× the threshold. Either way, the missing input is valid_fraction (or the raw valid-row count) — SparseCore's win comes from skipping invalid rows in its pipeline, and that's exactly the quantity the current heuristic never looks at. Production traffic on this model hasn't hit that gap yet only because topk=8 keeps it out of range — the fix is still worth making for any config where that stops being true.
