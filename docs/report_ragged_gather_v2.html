The Unconditional Path

ragged_gather_v2 has no performance dispatch at all — when SparseCore hardware is present, it always uses it; the only fallback to x[indices] is for hardware absence, never for speed. Benchmarking the two directly, on identical inputs, across a 12×11 grid of out_size (the exact XLA compile-bucket sizes seen in real MoE-permute traffic, 128 to 262,144) and window_fraction (the [start, end) coverage the kernel already exploits, 2% to 100%), shows the unconditional choice is wrong for a real slice of production calls — dense wins outright at every out_size ≤ 2,048, and even at the dominant real bucket it loses once the active window crosses a hard block-count boundary.

10.19% of 429,312 real ragged_gather_v2 calls, captured from a live vllm serve of Qwen3-30B-A3B (EP=8), land on inputs where dense x[indices] measured faster than the SparseCore path that always runs
3.12× worst measured slowdown — out_size=8,192 at full window: 0.142ms SparseCore vs 0.046ms dense
100% of calls at out_size ≤ 2,048 (6.98% of all real calls) hit sizes where dense wins outright, at every window fraction tested — no exceptions
1,024 rows the fixed pipeline block size once out_size passes 4× the base block — num_row_subchunks caps at 4, so bigger inputs get more blocks, not bigger ones

What the sweep shows

01

Below out_size=2,048, dense wins every single cell — window_fraction never matters

For out_size ∈ {128, 256, 512, 1,024, 2,048}, the SparseCore pipeline's block size (row_chunk_size × num_cores, driven by num_row_subchunks) works out to ≥ out_size itself on this hardware — the kernel can never use more than one pipeline block no matter where end lands, so window_fraction has zero effect on SparseCore's cost at these sizes. It still loses to dense every time, by 1.7×–2.4×, purely on fixed per-call SparseCore overhead that a single block can't amortize.

02

At the dominant real bucket, out_size=4,096, the crossover isn't smooth — it's a hard step at window=1,024

On this v6e-8 (SparseCore: 8 lanes × 2 cores × 16 subcores), the base block is 256 rows and num_row_subchunks caps at 4, so out_size=4,096 compiles to a fixed 1,024-row block size. Timing confirms it precisely: the dense÷SC ratio sits flat at ≈1.00× for every window ≤ 1,024 (1 block, true parity, not a near-miss), then drops to a flat 0.72× the instant window crosses 1,024 into a second block, 0.56× at a third, and 0.46× at a fourth (the full 4,096-row window). There is no gradual crossing here, only edges.

03

Because block size stops scaling past the cap, the largest sizes are where SparseCore's real advantage lives

Past out_size=4,096, num_row_subchunks stays pinned at 4 and block size stays pinned at 1,024 rows — so a 64× larger input (out_size=262,144) just gets 64× more blocks, not bigger ones. That's exactly the regime SparseCore's pipelining is built for: at low window_fraction it beats dense there by up to 64×, and even at a full window (window_fraction=100%) it's still ≥2× faster at every out_size ≥ 65,536 tested. The unconditional choice is right at this end of the grid — it's only wrong at the two ends the real workload actually spends time in.

04

Nearly all traffic funnels into a single out_size bucket — and that's exactly where the step boundary bites

90.79% of real calls compile to out_size=4,096 (the max_num_batched_tokens-driven shape). Its real window_fraction distribution: mean 12.5%, median 11.7%, p90 20.8%, p99 33.4% — comfortably under the 25% block boundary at the median and p90, but the tail already crosses it. 13,643 calls in this one bucket (3.50% of the bucket, 3.18% of everything logged) had window > 1,024, meaning SparseCore was measurably the wrong pick for them — and there is no code path that could have routed them elsewhere.

05

Outside that dominant bucket, small out_size traffic is a clean, total loss

6.98% of all real calls land on out_size ≤ 2,048, mostly the 128-row bucket (5.99%, from partial-batch prefill/decode steps). Every one of those runs SparseCore, and on this grid every one of them would have been faster as a plain dense gather. Combined with the out_size=4,096 tail, the actionable footprint is 43,750 calls — 10.19% of everything logged, in one 5m45s run, none of it currently reachable because there's no branch to change.

Reading on this

ragged_gather_v2 doesn't have a dispatch condition to fix — it needs one added. The data suggests the same two-part shape as the sibling reduce kernel: below out_size ≈ 4× the SparseCore base block (≈1,024 rows on this hardware — the point where num_row_subchunks can't yet reach its cap of 4), route to dense unconditionally, since SparseCore is paying fixed dispatch overhead for parallelism a single pipeline block can't extract. Above that, window_fraction and out_size stop being separately informative once num_row_subchunks saturates at 4: what actually matters is the number of active row-blocks, ceil((end−start) / 1,024) on this hardware, not the ratio to out_size. A window under one block is free money for SparseCore; every additional block bought below dense's flat per-call cost is a step the wrong way. Given real out_size is de facto fixed at 4,096 for 91% of this workload's traffic, even a narrow guard scoped to that one compiled shape — route to dense whenever end−start > 1,024 — would clear the entire measured 3.18% tail without touching the 96.5% majority that's already correctly served by SparseCore.
