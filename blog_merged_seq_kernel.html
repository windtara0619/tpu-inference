01 — The Problem

Short Sequences Starve the MXU

Embedding models process thousands of short queries simultaneously — search indices, RAG pipelines, and recommendation systems routinely handle 4–32 token inputs at scale. The problem is that the standard attention kernel treats every sequence as an independent tile.

For a 4-token sequence, the active compute region is a 4 × 4 = 16-element tile. The TPU v6e MXU native tile is 128 × 128 = 16 384 elements. That's less than 0.1% utilization per kernel call — regardless of how many sequences are in the batch.

Fig 1 · RPAm launches a separate kernel per sequence. The orange square is the active compute region. Everything else is idle MXU capacity.

02 — The Solution

One Tile, Many Sequences

RPAmerged packs a group of consecutive sequences into a single COMPUTE_SIZE × COMPUTE_SIZE tile. Their Q and KV vectors are concatenated, and a block-diagonal causal mask ensures each sequence only attends to its own tokens.

Because the entire group fits in one tile, flash attention's iterative accumulators — the running log-sum-exp normalizer l and the partial output buffer o — are no longer needed. A single Q × KT matmul covers all KV at once, and the output is written directly from softmax(S) × V without any cross-chunk rescaling. This removes two VMEM buffers and simplifies the inner loop significantly.

Fig 2 · RPAmerged packs sequences into one tile. Each coloured block is one sequence's causal attention region — cross-sequence logits are masked to −∞.

04 — Key Insights

What the Numbers Tell Us

Attention was 81% of TPU time — now it's 35%. At len=4/MERGE=0, RPAm consumes 107 of 132 ms of model compute. With RPAmerged it drops to 14 of 40 ms. The FFN now dominates the TPU budget.

The system is now CPU-bound at short sequences. At len=4/MERGE=1, the ~30 ms CPU overhead (prepare_inputs, scheduling, host↔device sync) accounts for 43% of total step time. The next gains must come from the host path, not the kernel.

Speedup diminishes with sequence length because the FFN scales with total tokens (26→94 ms for len=4→16) while RPAmerged saves a fixed ~93 ms of attention regardless of length. At len=64+, the FFN will dominate even MERGE=1.

Eliminating l and o simplifies the inner loop. Standard flash attention maintains running buffers for the log-sum-exp normalizer (l) and the rescaled partial output (o) across KV chunks. Since RPAmerged fits the entire KV for a group in one VMEM tile, there is only one chunk — so both buffers are dropped entirely. The output is written directly from the single-pass P × V product, removing two VMEM allocations and their associated read-modify-write cycles.

Conclusion

What's Next

RPAmerged demonstrates that significant throughput gains for short-sequence embedding workloads are achievable at the kernel level — no model changes, no API changes. By packing sequences into a block-diagonal MXU tile we deliver a 7.4× reduction in per-call attention time and a 1.77× end-to-end throughput improvement for 4-token sequences.

The remaining headroom is on the CPU host: the ~30 ms per-step overhead from _prepare_inputs, metadata construction, and host↔device synchronization is now 43% of total step time at short sequences. Reducing it — through pipelining host work with TPU execution or batching metadata operations — is the natural next target for this workload class.
